# Style 5 Isometric Infrastructure v3 Design

## Goal

Qualify `isometric-infrastructure` as a stable v3 article-visual style pack that turns supported service ecosystems and operational paths into polished editorial architectural dioramas.

The pack must stabilize camera, scale, material, spacing, light, and path hierarchy without allowing a golden image to authorize article-specific services, connections, ownership, or topology.

## Selected Direction

Use an editorial architectural diorama rather than a dense cloud campus or abstract pipeline sculpture.

The result should resemble a high-quality technology-magazine infrastructure illustration:

- a small number of substantial modules;
- a fixed and coherent spatial world;
- restrained matte materials;
- one clearly visible operational journey;
- enough empty ground to preserve hierarchy.

It must not resemble a cloud-vendor marketing page, a miniature server city, or a children's construction-toy scene.

## Visual Identity

The required visual grammar is:

- fully opaque pale gray matte ground based on `#EEF2F4`;
- fixed 30-degree isometric camera with consistent projection across every module;
- restrained blue modules based on `#4B8FD8`;
- one amber active path based on `#E8A43A`;
- crisp dark edges based on `#243746`;
- inactive or supporting modules based on `#AABAC5`;
- one soft directional light and one shared shadow direction;
- consistent module scale, platform thickness, corner treatment, and elevation logic;
- medium-low spatial density with generous outer margins.

The style must not contain:

- random perspective or mixed camera angles;
- dense miniature server racks, city clutter, or many tiny modules;
- glossy plastic, transparent glass, neon glow, or photorealistic materials;
- multiple competing routes, multiple amber accents, or decorative pipes;
- vendor logos, product branding, interface screenshots, or generated labels;
- unsupported modules, connections, ownership, importance, or hierarchy.

## Neighbor Boundary

The nearest neighbor is Style 4, `blueprint-linework`.

Style 5 uses a fixed isometric camera, matte solid modules, meaningful elevation, and directional shadows. Style 4 is flat, orthographic, line-led, and almost volumeless. Any front-orthographic cutaway, drafting grid, construction-line aesthetic, or dimension-like annotation should fail Style 5 qualification.

Style 5 must also remain distinct from Style 3, `neon-systems`. It uses pale matte daylight, restrained saturation, and no glow. Neon backgrounds, luminous circuitry, transparent glass, and dramatic dark atmosphere belong to Style 3.

## Golden Roles

### Cover

Create one complete isometric environment containing three to five substantial modules and one visible source-to-destination journey. The spatial world should occupy most of the wide canvas while preserving one deterministic title-safe area.

The amber journey must remain continuous, dominant, and understandable at thumbnail size. Modules may imply neutral services or stages through form, but they may not include labels, logos, screens, server-rack detail, or invented vendor-specific anatomy.

### Concept

Use a different topic and module family from the cover. Explain exactly one ownership, handoff, containment, or processing relation through two to four substantial modules and one supported path.

Scale and elevation may clarify the confirmed relation but may not invent importance. The cover reference contributes only camera, material, scale, light, density, and path visibility.

### Deterministic Diagram

Render only the article-confirmed frozen graph as a human-free isometric system. Every node and edge receives a stable identifier. Projection, platform thickness, edge treatment, plaque zones, and shadow direction are deterministic.

The golden diagram's example modules, labels, edges, groups, elevations, and topology are non-authoritative. Elevation may encode hierarchy only when explicitly present in the frozen semantics.

## Reference Contract

Each same-role golden may contribute only observable style properties:

- 30-degree isometric projection;
- module scale and shape abstraction;
- matte material and edge language;
- ground-plane behavior and shared lighting;
- spatial density, margins, crop behavior, and single-path hierarchy.

Every compiled prompt must forbid copying golden labels, numbers, nodes, topology, and example story.

During golden production, an approved earlier same-profile candidate may serve as a non-semantic bootstrap reference. It never authorizes services, connections, routes, elevation meaning, or article facts.

## Qualification Strategy

Qualification requires:

1. Three user-approved golden assets: cover, concept, and deterministic diagram.
2. A `visual-dna.json`, `role-contracts.json`, and `reference-matrix.json` pinned by SHA-256 in `golden-set.json`.
3. Successful compilation and linting for unrelated cover, concept, and diagram probes.
4. Explicit rejection of Style 4 orthographic blueprint language and Style 3 neon/glass language.
5. Release validation with all golden hashes, approval state, and qualification fields passing.

## Failure Handling

Reject and revise any candidate that:

- looks like a toy city, server farm, vendor marketing scene, or generic stock cloud illustration;
- contains too many small modules or lacks one dominant path;
- mixes camera angles, module scales, shadow directions, or material treatments;
- uses amber decoratively or creates multiple competing routes;
- invents services, connections, labels, logos, screens, or topology;
- is transparent, excessively empty, tightly cropped, or illegible at article width.

User approval remains image-specific. Rejected images never become references, and the contract is not weakened merely to make a candidate pass.

## Scope

This phase changes only Style 5 contracts, goldens, evaluation records, regression probes, registry qualification, and the tests or documentation required to support them. It does not reopen Style 4, redesign other modes, alter article semantics, or change publication Markdown formatting.
