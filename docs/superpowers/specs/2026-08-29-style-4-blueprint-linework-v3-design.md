# Style 4 Blueprint Linework v3 Design

## Goal

Qualify `blueprint-linework` as a stable v3 article-visual style pack whose cover, concept, and deterministic diagram outputs remain recognizably orthographic blueprint drawings across unrelated article topics.

The pack must reduce visual drift without allowing a golden image to become semantic authority. Article-specific facts, labels, nodes, edges, dimensions, and topology always come from confirmed article semantics.

## Selected Direction

Use the deep-blue blueprint ground as the sole golden-reference identity. Keep warm drafting paper only as an allowed non-golden inversion.

Use role-distinct golden examples rather than repeating one subject across all three roles:

- The cover proves large-scale orthographic cutaway composition.
- The concept image proves one boundary, layer, or component relationship on a different topic.
- The diagram proves exact human-free topology using deterministic rendering.

This separation tests whether the style transfers without copying the example story.

## Visual Identity

The required visual grammar is:

- deep blueprint-blue ground based on `#123B66`;
- fully opaque output with a restrained low-intensity drafting grid;
- uniform approximately 2 px pale construction and final-structure lines;
- sparse cyan based on `#72D5FF` for exactly one supported semantic boundary, layer, or transition;
- orthographic elevation, plan, section, cutaway, or exploded alignment;
- flat, line-led construction with measured spacing and almost no perceived volume;
- deterministic annotation zones reserved for later overlays.

The style must not contain:

- isometric or perspective camera angles;
- dramatic lighting, glow, directional shadows, glossy materials, or photorealism;
- fake dimensions, invented tolerances, pseudo-engineering identifiers, or decorative measurements;
- dense crosshatching or ornamental machinery;
- unsupported nodes, edges, ports, layers, or connections;
- AI-generated technical prose when deterministic overlay is required.

## Neighbor Boundary

The nearest neighbor is Style 5, `isometric-infrastructure`.

Style 4 remains flat, orthographic, line-led, and almost volumeless. Style 5 uses a fixed isometric camera, matte solids, spatial depth, directional shadows, and ecosystem-scale objects. Any visible 30-degree isometric projection, material volume, or scenic infrastructure should fail Style 4 qualification.

The warm drafting-paper inversion must also remain visibly distinct from Style 7. It may invert the surface but cannot adopt soft graphite variability, translucent watercolor washes, familiar-object teaching metaphors, or loose hand-drawn contours.

## Golden Roles

### Cover

Create one large, content-rich orthographic cutaway that explains a supported outside-boundary-inside thesis. The subject should occupy most of the wide canvas while preserving one clean title-safe area. Cyan identifies only the thesis boundary or transition.

The cover must be a complete technical picture, not empty grid paper with a small decorative schematic. It must not contain dimensions, labels, identifiers, or decorative components.

### Concept

Use a different topic and object family from the cover. Explain exactly one boundary, layer, or component relation through one main outline plus at most one ghosted construction or cutaway layer.

The image must remain understandable without generated labels. It may vary the object and arrangement while preserving projection, line discipline, grid restraint, and single-highlight behavior.

### Deterministic Diagram

Render the article-confirmed frozen graph as a fully human-free, front-orthographic architecture or flow diagram. Every node and edge receives a stable identifier. Node count, grouping, labels, and topology come only from the frozen graph.

Use pale final-structure lines, muted construction or group boundaries, and one cyan thesis feature only when supported. Arrows are orthogonal, uniform, and unglowed. The golden diagram's example labels, nodes, edges, and topology are non-authoritative.

## Reference Contract

Each same-role golden reference may contribute only observable style properties:

- projection and camera discipline;
- line weight and construction-line hierarchy;
- surface, grid, palette roles, density, and spacing;
- role-specific occupancy and annotation-zone behavior.

Every compiled prompt must explicitly forbid copying golden labels, numbers, nodes, topology, and example story.

Cover and concept generation may use the approved prior same-profile role as a bootstrap style reference during golden production, but it has no semantic authority. The deterministic diagram uses the validated frozen graph as its only topology authority.

## Qualification Strategy

Qualification requires:

1. Three user-approved golden assets: cover, concept, and diagram.
2. A `visual-dna.json`, `role-contracts.json`, and `reference-matrix.json` whose hashes are pinned by `golden-set.json`.
3. Successful prompt compilation and linting for three unrelated cross-topic probes.
4. Explicit discrimination from Style 5, including rejection of isometric camera, matte spatial solids, and directional shadow language.
5. Release validation with all golden hashes, approval state, and qualification fields passing.

## Failure Handling

Reject and revise any candidate that:

- looks isometric, cinematic, glossy, or photorealistic;
- uses cyan decoratively rather than semantically;
- appears empty or too small to function as a complete article image;
- invents engineering detail or generated technical text;
- copies another golden asset's subject or topology;
- contains transparency when the output contract requires an opaque article image.

User approval remains image-specific. A rejected candidate never becomes a reference and does not weaken the contract to make the image pass.

## Scope

This phase changes only Style 4 assets, v3 contracts, regression probes, tests, registry qualification, and design or implementation documentation needed for the work. It does not redesign other styles, change publication Markdown formatting, or modify article semantics.
